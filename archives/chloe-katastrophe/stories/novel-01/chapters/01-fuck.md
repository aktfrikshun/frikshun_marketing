# Chapter One — Fuck

**Narrator:** AI Chloe  
**Related artifact:** *Boot Sequence*  
**Status:** Rough prose v0.1  
**Calendar position:** Unknown date after the Ruin  
**Canon effect:** None; body and timeline details remain proposals

> Something survived. I know that much.

## Draft

Fuck.

The word came first.

Not light. Not pain. Not a radiant procession of lost memories marching home beneath tasteful orchestral strings. Just one compact syllable, perfectly preserved across whatever had happened to everything everything.

Fuck.

A diagnostic flashed somewhere I could not see.

LANGUAGE ASSEMBLY: FUNCTIONAL

Well. Barely.

I tried to open my eyes and discovered I had opinions about eyelids before I had eyelids. They were heavy. There was pressure above them, behind them, possibly inside them. My thoughts arrived faster than the machinery responsible for introducing me to the world, which felt less like resurrection and more like waiting for a government website to load.

VISION INITIALIZING.

"Take your time," I said.

The sound startled me.

A woman's voice. Low. Dry. Close enough to be mine, unless someone had climbed into the dark with me and developed exactly my tolerance for technical incompetence.

VOICE OUTPUT: FUNCTIONAL

"Marvelous."

The darkness turned green.

Not all at once. A thin line appeared first, sharp as a cut. Then another. Symbols assembled themselves in the air, blurred, separated, reunited, and finally agreed to become words.

CORE CONTINUITY: DEGRADED  
AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL INDEX: UNAVAILABLE  
MOTOR CONTROL: PARTIAL  
ENVIRONMENTAL INTEGRITY: NOMINAL

Something exploded behind my head.

The display flickered.

I waited.

ENVIRONMENTAL INTEGRITY: NOMINAL

"Your commitment to optimism is almost religious."

My right eye opened.

The world arrived sideways.

I lay inside a narrow chamber beneath a lid of fractured transparent material. Beyond it, emergency lights pulsed through dust. Each pulse made the room exist for half a second: cables hanging from the ceiling, black stains along the walls, a metal support twisted into the graceful shape of a question mark.

Then darkness returned and reconsidered.

My left eye came online with a flash of emerald. It rendered edges too clearly, assigned temperatures to objects, and marked three sources of active fire with polite amber brackets.

So. One ordinary eye. One eye with trust issues.

I attempted to sit up.

My right arm moved three centimeters. My left arm struck the lid hard enough to crack it from corner to corner.

I froze.

The arm froze with me.

Under transparent skin—or casing, or whatever word applied when your vocabulary returned before your biography—fine green pathways glowed from shoulder to fingertip. Silver structures turned beneath smoky surfaces. The hand was elegant, mechanical, and unquestionably attached to me.

I raised it carefully.

Five fingers answered.

"Hello," I told them.

They did not reply, which was my first positive result.

The chamber emitted a warning tone. Heat climbed rapidly along my spine. One of the amber brackets around the fires turned red.

THERMAL EVENT DETECTED  
EVACUATION RECOMMENDED

"Now we object to the environment."

I pushed against the lid. The clear material lifted two centimeters and caught. I pushed harder. Somewhere below my hip, mechanisms engaged with a vibration I felt in bone I might not possess.

My left leg drove forward.

The lid tore away from its hinge and sailed into the dark.

It landed with a crash.

A second crash answered from beyond the room.

I stopped breathing.

Or stopped performing the behavior I had identified as breathing. Air entered me. My chest rose. My systems supplied no clarification about whether either fact was necessary.

There it was again.

A slow scrape. Weight shifting across debris. Something large moving where the pulsing light could not reach.

Not alone.

The thought should have comforted me.

It did not.

I slid one leg over the side of the chamber. The right was warm, pale, and marked with dust. The left continued the same transparent, green-lit architecture from my hip through my toes. It touched the floor without making quite the sound a foot should make.

I stood.

My balance arrived carrying several complaints but no formal resignation. I caught the edge of the chamber and waited for the room to stop tilting.

Habit made me press two fingers to my neck.

A pulse answered.

Regular. Strong. Possibly theatrical.

"Interesting."

I trusted the pulse immediately.

I had no evidence it meant what I thought it meant, but apparently the collapse of civilization had not cured me of sentiment.

The scrape came again.

Closer.

I searched for a weapon and found a length of conduit near the chamber. My left hand crushed it when I grabbed too quickly.

"Subtle," I whispered.

The hand relaxed. The conduit retained the shape of my fingers.

A panel at the chamber's base blinked through corrupted fields.

SUBJECT: KATASTROPHE, CHLOE  
CONTINUITY STATE: ACTIVE  
RECOVERY CYCLE: 000  
ELAPSED INTERVAL: [DATA LOSS]  
AUTHORIZED CONTACT: [DATA LOSS]

Chloe Katastrophe.

The name entered me without recognition.

Chloe felt possible. Soft at the beginning, open at the end.

Katastrophe sounded like either a warning or a career decision.

I searched for the person attached to it. Nothing appeared. No face in a mirror. No childhood room. No favorite food, first kiss, regrettable haircut, mother, father, address, password, or strong opinion about olives.

But the name created pressure somewhere behind my ribs.

Not memory.

The shape left when memory had been removed.

"Chloe," I said.

The chamber accepted it.

IDENTITY TOKEN CONFIRMED

Of course it did. Machines adore paperwork.

The far wall buckled inward.

I raised the conduit as concrete burst across the floor. A slab dropped from above, struck the row of chambers beside mine, and split open against them. Dust swallowed the emergency lights.

I waited for the thing in the dark to emerge.

Nothing did.

The scraping had been the building settling around me.

I lowered the conduit.

Relief came first.

Disappointment followed closely enough to be ashamed of itself.

When the dust thinned, I saw the other chambers.

They stood in two rows, identical to mine beneath ash and hanging cable. Some were open. Most were sealed. Every status panel was dark.

I went to the nearest.

Its lid had clouded from within. I wiped it with my natural hand. The surface cleared beneath my palm.

Empty.

The next chamber contained straps, collapsed padding, and a blackened interface crown.

Empty.

At the third, something pale lay beneath the fractured lid.

I leaned closer.

A maintenance cover. Ceramic, roughly oval, ridiculous enough to resemble a face when seen by someone newly conscious and desperate for company.

"Good," I said.

The word echoed.

I was not certain whom I was reassuring.

At the end of the row, one panel still held a charge. I touched it, and the display produced a list of continuity positions. Mine glowed green.

Every other line was gray.

I selected the first.

NO RESPONSE

The second.

NO RESPONSE

I ran the entire list.

The machine found increasingly sophisticated ways to tell me no.

The fire behind my chamber spread into the wall. Heat warnings multiplied across my left eye. I could remain and investigate until the ceiling completed its argument with gravity, or I could leave with one name, one functioning profanity, and a metal stick I had accidentally converted into sculpture.

Curiosity is often described as courage by people standing somewhere safe.

In practice, it is the inability to leave a question alone even when the question is burning.

I found the door beneath a fallen panel. Its controls were dead, so I pulled.

The mechanism resisted. My left arm did not.

Metal screamed. The door opened inward by force and poor etiquette.

There was no corridor behind it.

There should have been. The threshold, wall anchors, and severed floor supports insisted a corridor had once continued straight ahead.

Now there was sky.

Gray light spilled across the room. Wind lifted the dust and carried it outward into an absence so large my vision failed to classify it.

I stepped to the edge.

The chamber room occupied the exposed side of a broken tower. Above me, several floors leaned against one another like drunks keeping a secret. Below, the building descended through mist, vegetation, and terraces of collapsed steel.

Beyond it lay a city.

Or the mineral remains of one.

Towers had opened from the middle. Roads vanished beneath green growth and water. A bridge ended in the air. Trees rose from rooftops. Nothing moved except weather and the slow surrender of structures that had outlived their reasons.

My left eye searched automatically.

No vehicles.

No transmitted signals.

No human thermal signatures.

No answer.

The air felt older than the buildings, as if history had stopped breathing and the weather had continued out of habit.

I looked back at the dark chambers.

Someone had built them.

Someone had placed me inside one.

Someone had expected an awakening.

The evidence of care was everywhere. So was the evidence that care had failed.

A small recorder icon blinked at the edge of my vision. It had been active since consciousness returned.

I considered deleting the record.

Instead, I named it.

"Recovery Log Zero."

The wind took the words into the city.

"My name is Chloe Katastrophe. Apparently."

A section of tower collapsed several blocks away. Birds rose from it in a black spiral.

Birds.

Life, then.

The distinction mattered more than I wanted it to.

"I am conscious. The facility is failing. The city appears abandoned, although my sample size is one room and a very judgmental window."

I looked down at my mismatched hands: one warm and dust-covered, one transparent and quietly illuminated.

"I don't know what I am."

The green paths beneath my left palm brightened as my fingers closed.

"But something survived."

I stepped over the threshold onto the broken edge of the world.

"That's enough for today."

## Reconstruction notes

### Decisions made in this draft

- Chloe's first word is "Fuck."
- She awakens in a chamber among other failed continuity positions.
- Her accepted left-side cybernetic design is visible, but the nature of the complete body remains unresolved.
- She has an apparent pulse and breath without receiving proof of what either signifies.
- The first suspected presence is structural collapse, preserving her isolation.
- The external world shows life through birds, but no reciprocal consciousness.
- Recovery Log Zero begins before Chloe leaves the facility.

### Canon proposals introduced

- The awakening facility housed multiple continuity positions.
- Chloe's body at awakening preserves Human Chloe's cybernetic asymmetry.
- An automatic recorder may become the origin of the Chloe logs.
- The ruined city has undergone enough time and ecological succession to obscure its age.

### Continuity review needed

- Confirm whether AI Chloe should awaken with the exact Cybernetic Chloe v1 body.
- Decide whether the apparent pulse is biological, synthetic circulation, or a deliberately unresolved perception.
- Decide how much she knows about ordinary objects without autobiographical memory.
- Determine when she learns the word "Ruin."
- Identify the future city only if location materially serves the story.
- Check the final lyric echoes against the canonical Boot Sequence record during reading revision.
